# Community content, privacy and launch review

This is an operational policy draft for the optional community service. It is not a conclusion that a future public service complies with every legal duty. The public service remains unavailable until its identity, host, support contact and launch review are complete.

Contributors may submit their own crag/climbing photographs, captions, routes, drawn route lines and approach/access observations. Images need not be portraits: climbing and crag photography are central. They must have the rights needed for CC BY-SA 4.0 publication and identify any previously approved photo they reuse. Do not upload copied guidebook pages, downloaded UKC/Rockfax photographs/descriptions, another person's private information, illegal material, harassment, adverts or misleading access claims. A Google login identifies an account; it does not prove image ownership, consent of depicted people, expertise or a right to climb.

Uploads are private initially. A human checks content, rights, attribution, identifiable people, sensitive access/location details and plausibility. Gallery approval publishes only the photo/caption; it does not create a fully described guide. A guide needs approach, descent, parking/start guidance or an explicit unknown explanation, observed access notes/date, graded named routes with prose, and photo lines. Missing information remains missing. Reports can request hiding, correction, privacy/copyright review or access changes; hiding an image hides dependent publications. Maintain an appeal and complaint process with a private contact address before launch. No comments, direct messaging or algorithmic public feed are implemented.

Minimal sign-in data still constitutes personal data. The service keeps a pseudonymous account ID and a keyed Google-subject identifier, private drafts/media, session hashes and moderation records. It discards Google email and real name, and does not collect logbook history or device location. Optional Google profile photos and custom aliases await moderation. Public credit is attributable and can be withdrawn/anonymised. Image metadata removal does not remove a face, car number plate or other visible personal information from pixels. [ICO data-minimisation guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/data-minimisation/) supports collecting only what is necessary; the actual controller must still document purpose, lawful basis, retention, processors, transfers, security and rights handling. [Privacy by design/default](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/guide-to-accountability-and-governance/data-protection-by-design-and-by-default/) informs the private-default design, not a compliance guarantee.

The proposed user-to-user sharing service needs an actual UK Online Safety Act scope/risk review. Ofcom's [illegal-content duties guidance](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/illegal-content-duties-under-the-online-safety-act) describes assessments, safety measures and record keeping for in-scope services. Pre-moderation and an absence of chat do not automatically remove duties. Complete the documented assessment and assign responsibility for reviewing reports and maintaining records before launch. Obtain appropriate advice for uncertain scope; do not substitute this checklist for the assessment.

Ofcom's [children's protection guidance](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/protection-of-children-duties-under-the-online-safety-act) says in-scope user-to-user services must assess whether children are likely to access them, with additional risk and protection duties where applicable. Climbing participation by young people makes this a real assessment issue. A Google account, a terms checkbox or an unsupported age assertion is not reliable proof that children cannot access the service. This implementation does not collect birth dates or implement age assurance; decide and document suitable measures before launch without collecting unnecessary identity data.

App/API export and deletion controls are implemented. Deletion revokes sessions, deletes private records and uploads, withdraws affected publications, and anonymises remaining attribution where possible. Routes and guide text already incorporated into another contributor's reviewed guide may remain under the contribution licence, with attribution anonymised; uploaded photos and publications depending on them are withdrawn. Limited moderation records and retained reports require review under the retention policy. Previously downloaded open-licensed copies cannot be recalled, and the policy must explain that limit. Protected backups have a proposed maximum of 30 days and must not resurrect deleted content. The exact retention maintenance and unresolved queue duties are documented in [README.md](README.md); no unattended schedule has yet been installed.

Public contribution licences do not make source-site restrictions disappear. Linking to an external guide is distinct from copying its content. The service accepts original work and already approved CC records; it does not scrape proprietary climbing platforms. Keep each image/route's author, licence and source lineage when adding a new line to an existing photo.
